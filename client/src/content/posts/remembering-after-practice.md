summary: An immediate correct answer and recall a week later describe different outcomes. Practice design needs room to observe both.

A learning activity can feel productive while producing a different result a week later.

In [Roediger and Karpicke’s 2006 experiments](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/), students read prose passages and then either studied them again or practised recalling them. Repeated study helped more on a test five minutes later. On tests two days or a week later, retrieval practice produced better retention.

## Interpreting immediate success

The students who studied repeatedly also became more confident in what they would remember. Their confidence and their later recall did not move together.

For a learning app, that makes the timing of a progress check important. A correct answer immediately after an explanation measures something different from remembering the material after a delay.

I would make room for both: support while a concept is new, then a later opportunity to recall and use it. This study was about remembering prose, so it does not settle every question about teaching. It does show why an immediate success score is an incomplete account of learning.

## What the progress indicator counts

A score shown at the end of a session can combine several kinds of success: remembering an explanation, recognizing an answer, following a hint or applying a concept independently. Treating them as the same event makes the score simpler, but less informative.

For a learner or parent, it helps to say what was observed. “Completed with hints” and “recalled independently after a delay” convey different information without making either result a judgment about the learner’s ability.

## Make room to return

A later practice opportunity does not have to be a high-stakes test. It can be a short prompt to recall an idea or use it in another example. The purpose is to observe what remains available after the immediate context has faded.

The cited experiments concerned remembering prose. They do not establish that one practice schedule is best for every subject or learner. Their practical value here is narrower: the timing and form of an assessment can change what it tells us, so an immediate success indicator should be read with that context.
