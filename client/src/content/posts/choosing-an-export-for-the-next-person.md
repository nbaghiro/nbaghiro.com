“Can I export it?” leaves quite a lot unanswered when choosing a presentation tool.

A PDF may be exactly what someone needs to read and approve a proposal. A colleague who needs to change the figures needs a different kind of handoff. A video serves someone who needs to watch a narrated presentation.

## Testing the handoff

[PowerPoint itself offers](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/powerpoint/export-a-presentation) PDF, video and Word handouts as separate export paths. Those formats serve different uses, even when they begin with the same slides.

I would test the handoff with a small, ordinary revision: change a number, edit a paragraph and add a slide in the receiving application. Check whether the text is editable, whether the chart still has usable data and whether the layout holds up.

A file that looks right on arrival can still be expensive to revise. Export support is more useful to assess through the recipient's next task than through a list of file extensions.

## Try an ordinary revision

The test does not need a complicated document. Use one with the things people actually change: a heading, a paragraph, a chart and an image. Open the exported file in the application the recipient will use, then make a small revision to each.

That exercise separates several questions. Does the file open? Does it look right? Can the recipient edit its contents in the expected way? A successful answer to the first question does not establish the other two. A chart that arrives as a picture may be suitable for approval and unsuitable for updating the numbers.

## Agree on the handoff

Before choosing a format, establish whether the recipient needs to read, revise, present or archive the work. There may be a reason to send both a reference PDF and an editable source file. The PDF records how the work was intended to appear; the source supports further changes.

That is also a useful basis for evaluating a new creation tool. A polished result inside its own editor is one part of the experience. The work after it leaves the editor belongs in the evaluation too.
