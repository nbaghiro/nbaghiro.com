OPTION 1: PERSONAL AND REFLECTIVE

I’ve realised I enjoy working on several products at once more than I expected.

I understand the appeal of putting all your attention into one thing. But moving between different problems keeps me interested, and something I learn in one app often helps with another.

We have several mobile apps ready to submit to the stores, which has me thinking about a small app studio. Each app would have its own purpose, with shared practices for testing, releases and support.

I’m still figuring out what that would look like as a business. More apps also mean more maintenance, marketing and decisions about where to spend time.

For now, I’m enjoying the work and curious to see which products people find useful. I’d like to give that a chance to shape what comes next.

OPTION 2: CASUAL AND A LITTLE PLAYFUL

Apparently, my idea of a change of pace is opening a different codebase.

I’ve been enjoying working on several products at once. A tricky problem in one app can give way to a small design improvement in another, and I often come back with a fresh idea.

We have several mobile apps ready to submit, so I’ve started thinking about organising them as a small app studio. Focused apps, each with a useful job to do, and a shared way to handle the work behind them.

I don’t know yet how well the business side will work. I’ll still need to make time for marketing, support and all the less exciting parts of keeping apps useful.

But I like this way of building. I want to see whether I can give it enough structure to keep doing it well.

A longer write-up of the idea:
https://nbaghiro.com/blogs/finding-a-rhythm-across-multiple-products

OPTION 3: THE STUDIO IDEA

I’m thinking about building a small mobile app studio.

We already have several apps ready to submit to the stores. Each addresses a problem we think is worth solving, and I’ve found that I really enjoy working across them.

The idea is to give each app a clear purpose and audience, while sharing the parts that benefit from consistency: testing, releases, support and maintenance.

I wouldn’t expect every app to get equal attention every week. I’m imagining one primary focus at a time, room to explore, and clear commitments to the products already in use.

There’s plenty I still need to learn, especially about reaching the right people and supporting several products well. If one app needs more attention, I want to be able to follow that.

I wrote about the approach, what appeals to me, and what I’m still figuring out:
https://nbaghiro.com/blogs/finding-a-rhythm-across-multiple-products
